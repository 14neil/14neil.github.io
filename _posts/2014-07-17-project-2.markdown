description: A tidal disruption event (TDE) occurs when a star approaching a supermassive black hole (SMBH) experiences a differential gravitational energy from its self-binding energy and the SMBH’s gravity. If the star orbits too closely to the SMBH, half of the star's mass is tidally unbound while the bound mass forms an accretion disk. The figure above is the light curve of TDE ASASSN-15oi, plotted with archival data taken with NASA's Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory. The shape of the TDE light curve is the result of the initial formation of the accretion disk emitting X-ray/UV photons. The excess late-time near-UV flux is thought to originate from a long-lasting accretion disk supplied by the outermost bound debris.
